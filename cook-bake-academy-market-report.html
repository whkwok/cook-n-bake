Cook & Bake Academy Singapore

Market pressure-test, fee benchmark and launch recommendation

Research date: 24 September 2026 · Currency: Singapore dollars (S$)

Decision report

Executive recommendation

Proceed with a narrow, test-led launch—not the full 20-course calendar. Launch five courses across two campuses, require paid deposits before committing each intake, and postpone advanced and long-duration programmes until the academy has reviews, repeat customers and evidence of conversion.

Pricing verdict. The three one-session offers (S$160–S$220) sit inside or just above the current commercial workshop market of roughly S$118–S$220 per person in the sampled live offers. Multi-week Cook & Bake Academy courses cost S$160–S$245 per assumed weekly lesson; this is generally above ABC Cooking Studio's current S$106.82–S$165.68 per lesson for comparable single-discipline packages and far above subsidised/public ITE short-course hourly pricing. The premium may be defensible through small classes of 12, specialist outcomes and continuity, but willingness to pay is UNKNOWN until tested with deposits.

1. Three working customer personas

The experience-led professional

Need: a rewarding evening/weekend activity, date or gift that feels more substantial than dining out.

Chooses on: location, visual result, instructor credibility, schedule and an all-inclusive experience.

Barriers: multi-week commitment, total price and fear of being too inexperienced.

Likely courses: BAK-108 Cookies, BAK-106 Cupcakes & Cake Pops, CUL-202 Thai, CUL-210 Knife Skills.

Inferred persona. Competitor positioning supports social/date/gift use cases, but age, income and budget are UNKNOWN.[S4][S6]

The serious home-skill builder

Need: repeatable technique and feedback that free videos cannot provide.

Chooses on: hands-on time, progression, class size, take-home output and transfer to a home kitchen.

Barriers: comparing the per-lesson price with packages and SkillsFuture-eligible alternatives.

Likely courses: BAK-101 Sourdough, BAK-107 Bread, CUL-201 Italian, CUL-204 French Foundations.

Inferred persona. ABC and ITE demonstrate multi-lesson and subsidised alternatives; measured willingness to pay is UNKNOWN.[S3][S5]

The outcome-driven specialist

Need: a portfolio piece, side-business capability or specialist technique.

Chooses on: instructor authority, advanced output, assessment/certificate, ingredient quality and commercial relevance.

Barriers: no recognised credential, prerequisite uncertainty and high total price.

Likely courses: BAK-102 Viennoiserie, BAK-103 Wedding Cake, BAK-105 Chocolate, CUL-203 Sushi.

Inferred persona. Professional/course-career demand exists as an adjacent market, but side-business conversion and budget are UNKNOWN.[S7]

4. Demand signals and seasonality

Direct or near-direct signals

  • Visible scarcity: Commune Kitchen labels its current Thai and dumpling listings “Only a few left”; this supports demand for approachable Asian social cooking at S$185, but seat capacity and starting inventory are UNKNOWN.[S4]
  • Confirmed supply: Palate Sensations shows four “Upcoming Confirmed Classes” priced S$90–S$195. This proves scheduled/confirmed activity, not attendance volume.[S2]
  • Active commercial calendar with discounts: ToTT advertises multiple Oct 2026 classes at S$118–S$168, with member and anniversary offers; it also says classes may be cancelled three days before delivery if minimum enrolment is not met. This simultaneously signals a functioning market and occupancy risk.[S1]
  • Public provision: ITE listed Oct–Nov 2026 culinary short courses at S$207.10–S$239.80 public fees and marked SkillsFuture Credit available. This is strong evidence of funded alternative supply, not measured category demand.[S5]

What remains UNKNOWN: market size, competitor enrolments, repeat rate, CAC, conversion rate, Academy occupancy and willingness to pay. None should be forecast from the above signals.

Seasonality and proposed-calendar implications

  • Festive making/gifting: ToTT has repeatedly marketed Christmas and Lunar New Year cooking/baking classes, supporting those themes as a recurring campaign pattern; the cited Christmas/CNY examples are historical, not proof of 2026 sales.[S9]
  • Holiday timing: Singapore has 11 gazetted public holidays in 2026. Christmas falls Friday 25 Dec 2026; Deepavali falls Sunday 8 Nov with Monday 9 Nov observed for relevant rest-day arrangements.[S10]
  • Catalogue conflicts under the weekly-session assumption: the 5 Dec four-week BAK-101 intake would include 26 Dec; the 19 Dec six-week BAK-103 intake would include 26 Dec and 2 Jan; the 11 Oct eight-week BAK-102 intake would include Deepavali on 8 Nov. The 28 Dec eight-week CUL-204 intake would include 8 Feb 2027, the observed day after 2027 Lunar New Year weekend. These dates should be rescheduled or explicitly confirmed before sale.
  • Tourist upside is only a proxy: Singapore recorded 16.9 million international visitor arrivals in 2025, up 2.3% from 2024. Tourist-ready one-off classes may benefit, but Orchard/Bukit Timah conversion is UNKNOWN without channel tests.[S11]

5. Five courses to launch first

  1. CUL-210 Knife Skills & Kitchen Essentials — S$160, one 3h session, Bukit Timah. Lowest-price acquisition course, broad beginner utility and market-consistent pricing. It can feed graduates into cuisine programmes. Risk: easy to compare with free content; sell live correction and sharpening. Test: two paid cohorts before adding more fundamentals.
  2. BAK-108 Cookie & Biscuit Baking — S$180, one 3h session, Orchard. Low commitment, take-home value and festive/gift adaptability; price sits inside Palate’s sampled range. Risk: crowded beginner category. Test: rotate a festive variant rather than adding separate SKUs.
  3. CUL-202 Thai Street Food — S$540, three 3h sessions, Bukit Timah. Closest direct evidence: Commune lists a 3h Thai class at S$185 with “Only a few left”; Academy’s S$180/session is nearly identical. Risk: S$540 upfront commitment versus buying one class. Test: offer a paid first-session credit toward the series.
  4. BAK-101 Artisan Sourdough — S$680, four 3h sessions, Orchard. Clear progression and a result that benefits from feedback over time; S$56.67/hour sits within the sampled commercial workshop band. Risk: S$170/lesson is 59% above ABC Bread Basic. Test: require deposits and document home-oven suitability.
  5. CUL-208 Vegetarian & Vegan Cuisine — S$540, three 3h sessions, Bukit Timah. Differentiated dietary proposition; its S$180/session sits inside WakaMama’s current S$138–S$220 group range and near Palate’s S$148 specialist vegan workshop. Risk: broad “vegan cuisine” may be less compelling than a precise cuisine/outcome. Test: market two menu concepts before finalising the syllabus.

Why the prominent alternatives should wait

BAK-102, BAK-103 and CUL-204 require S$1,280–S$1,580 and six to eight weeks before the Academy has trust signals. CUL-203 has the catalogue’s highest per-session price (S$245) plus raw-fish safety and ingredient-cost exposure. CUL-209 is above the sampled group-class price ceiling and adds charcoal/site complexity. BAK-104/105/109 and CUL-201/205/206/207 are plausible second-wave courses, but lack equally strong direct subject-price evidence. BAK-106 is a simple entry format priced at the top of the sampled group market. BAK-107 and BAK-110 overlap the recommended sourdough launch; avoid splitting initial bread demand.

Launch validation gates

  • Publish landing pages for only the five recommendations and measure paid deposits by course, campus, time and source channel.
  • Confirm session count, hands-on minutes, GST treatment, refund rules, ingredient inclusions and instructor credentials before public comparison.
  • Set a minimum viable cohort from actual instructor, ingredient and campus costs; break-even enrolment is currently UNKNOWN.
  • Interview depositors and non-buyers separately; stated interest without payment is not price validation.
  • Do not open both published intakes automatically. Release the second only after the first reaches the internally calculated threshold.
